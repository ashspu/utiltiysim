01 / Generative zoning proposal

Two rivers, wooded buffers, organic neighbourhoods, a main street, school, church and industrial district. The apparent lot marks are illustrative, not a building census.

02 / Measured interpretation

Roads are manually interpreted from the proposal, then planarised into one connected graph. The natural parcel capacity is 451 homes, within the approved 450–550 range. Reserved sites and rivers are hard constraints.

03 / Authoritative parcels and footprints

476 private/service premises: 451 homes, 16 shops, school, church, six industrial sites and water works. Every building has its own system mapping. Exactly 100 pool polygons belong to specific home IDs.

04 / Live system identities

Every property belongs to one of 27 street blocks and resolves to a parcel, building, account and service records. Accounts are not assumed to be interchangeable with parcels. The localhost world has 1,384 meters and seven saved days.

05a / First vector presentation

Retained before-image: driveway lines mistakenly ended at road centre lines. The image model reproduced that defect, showing why geometry errors must be fixed before generation.

05b / Corrected vector presentation

Driveway polygons are cut at the pavement boundary. 164 selected saved poles use roughly 42 m spacing targets and a town-wide 35 m minimum clearance, including corners. 54 saved hydrants are illustrated at the curb; their original hydraulic network coordinates are retained separately.

06a / First full-town AI trial

The broad composition survives, but some roofs and campus footprints grow or change. Painted pool count and exact roof registration are not certified. The model returned 1,536 × 1,024 pixels, despite a higher-resolution request.

06b / Targeted curb cleanup

A second image edit removes driveway bars from the roadway. This improves the presentation but is not proof that all artwork pixels match the measured geometry.

07a / Measured neighbourhood source

A 240 × 160 map-unit crop rendered as a 3,072 × 2,048 reference. Pools, roofs, curbs and furniture remain exact vector objects.

07b / Higher-detail generative pilot

The same crop generated separately gives 6.4 pixels per map unit, compared with 1 pixel per unit for the whole-town image. It preserves much of the neighbourhood character but enlarges and reshapes some roofs.

07c / Corrected neighbourhood pilot

Targeted driveway repair. Compare the painted roof edges and pool edges with the mapped outlines in the interactive viewer; visual registration is still an open quality gate.

08 / Working map and records

The live viewer exposes all 476 property regions and 103 road segments. Road pavement and hit targets are generated from the same saved centreline and width.

09 / Street detail evidence

Native vector close-up: corrected accesses, spaced poles and curbside hydrants. Wires are intentionally absent.

10 / Whole-town hybrid

Generated image under authoritative selectable parcels. This remains an experimental illustration layer, not a geometry-certified raster.

11 / Pilot hybrid

Separate neighbourhood detail layer with the same parcel coordinates, plus deterministic street furniture. This is the high-detail comparison view.

12 / Alignment audit

Gold marks the saved road pavement; cyan marks recorded pools. This exposes where the generated illustration diverges from authoritative geometry.

13 / Click through to saved records

A property selected on the illustrated layer resolves to its source IDs and verified saved utility history.
